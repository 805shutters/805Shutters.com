# Commercial sales launch plan

Date: 2026-06-08

## Goal

Create a repeatable commercial pipeline for 805 Shutters across Ventura County
and nearby markets, focused on offices, storefronts, schools, medical spaces,
tenant improvements, property managers, and general contractors.

The first target is not a single large public bid. The first target is a steady
stream of bid invites, site walks, shade audits, and small commercial wins that
turn into repeat work.

## Core offer

Free commercial shade audit:

- Glare, heat, privacy, and security review.
- Safety review for corded or damaged window coverings.
- Replacement plan by priority, room, or phase.
- Budget range before final measuring.
- Fast site measure and clean install coordination.

Lead with roller shades, solar shades, blackout shades, motorized shades,
vertical blinds, replacement blinds, and safety-compliant options. Shutters can
stay available, but they should not be the lead commercial product.

## Target accounts

Prioritize these groups in this order:

1. Tenant-improvement general contractors.
2. Commercial property managers.
3. Schools, colleges, and public agencies.
4. Medical, dental, and professional offices.
5. Architects, designers, and office-interior vendors.
6. Storefronts, restaurants, hospitality, and shared spaces.

## 30-day execution plan

### Week 1 - Build the sales kit

- Finalize the capability sheet in `content/commercial/commercial-capability-sheet.md`.
- Pick 8-12 installed-product photos, with at least 3 commercial or office-like photos.
- Fill out the company facts block: license, insurance, service radius, brands,
  turnaround expectations, and best commercial references.
- Add the free commercial shade audit offer to the commercial landing page.
- Add the commercial lead source values to the CRM or lead tracker.

### Week 2 - Create the first target list

- Add 50 accounts to the private CRM or internal lead tracker.
- Use this starting mix:
  - 20 tenant-improvement GCs and commercial remodelers.
  - 15 property managers and commercial real estate managers.
  - 5 school, college, or local agency procurement offices.
  - 5 architects, designers, or office-furniture vendors.
  - 5 direct office/medical/storefront prospects.
- Register or prepare registration for the portals in
  `content/commercial/bid-portal-checklist.csv`.

### Week 3 - Start outreach

- Send 10 GC/estimator emails.
- Call 10 property managers.
- Send 5 designer/architect intros.
- Drop off 3 capability sheets in person.
- Check public/bid portals twice.
- Log every touch, next step, and owner in the private CRM or internal lead tracker.

### Week 4 - Convert conversations into site walks

- Follow up every untouched account from week 3.
- Ask every GC: "Who handles Division 12 window treatments on your TI bids?"
- Ask every property manager: "Do you have a current shade/blind replacement
  vendor for vacancies, glare issues, or damaged coverings?"
- Book site walks before quoting whenever possible.
- Send same-day recap after every walk with options, rough range, and next step.

## Weekly operating cadence

Every Monday:

- Review open opportunities and due dates.
- Pick 25 accounts for the week.
- Assign owner and next action.

Every Tuesday to Thursday:

- 5-8 outbound touches per day.
- Same-day reply handling.
- Quote/site-walk follow-up before new prospecting.

Every Friday:

- Update status, next action, and close reason.
- Review bid portals.
- Count new contacts, conversations, site walks, proposals, and wins.

## Success metrics

Track these weekly:

- New commercial contacts added.
- First touches sent.
- Replies or live conversations.
- Site walks booked.
- Bid list/vendor registrations submitted.
- Commercial proposals sent.
- Commercial jobs won.
- Average proposal value.
- Top loss reason.

Early target for the first 60-90 days:

- 10 or more bid lists joined.
- 5 or more site walks.
- 10 or more commercial proposals.
- 1-3 small commercial wins.
- 2 repeat buyer relationships identified.

## Qualification rules

Good opportunities:

- Decision-maker or estimator identified.
- Site walk available or plans/specs available.
- Product need is clear: glare, privacy, heat, safety, damage, vacancy,
  remodel, motorization, or replacement.
- Buyer can define timing and approval path.
- Job fits service area and install capacity.

Weak opportunities:

- No decision-maker.
- No budget, no plans, and no site access.
- Pure price-shopping without scope clarity.
- Install timing is incompatible with supplier lead times.
- The buyer expects free estimating on large plan sets without any relationship
  or chance of repeat work.
